I work on AI for science. I trained as an analytical chemist and worked on NASA’s Curiosity rover.

I am co-founder and CEO of Deleon Technologies, an AI research company that measures human biochemistry every day, inside living people, to train models of human metabolism. We built the Otea, a portable chemical analyzer that runs in a participant's home at about 1% of the cost of commercial instruments, and Exploreon, a model that chooses the most informative next experiment for each person.

Before Deleon I worked on the search for life beyond Earth. At NASA Goddard I was part of the Curiosity rover's Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM) team, where I ran the Earth testbed experiments behind the rover's search for organic molecules in Gale crater. At Georgetown, with the NASA Laboratory for Agnostic Biosignatures, I worked on how to recognize life without assuming it uses Earth's biochemistry. My PhD in analytical chemistry at Georgia Tech, in Amanda Stockton's lab, studied how salt hides and preserves the molecular evidence of life, with field work from the Orca Basin brine pool in the Gulf of Mexico to the acid lakes of Western Australia.

I am also applying methods from life detection to AI agents, such as finding self-replicating prompts in agent swarms.

Outside of work, I'm a Buddhist, I'm trying to get better at surfing, and I love alpine climbing. I'm also into aviation and photography. My photography collection is for sale; email me at chad.ian.poz@gmail.com to ask about a print.
