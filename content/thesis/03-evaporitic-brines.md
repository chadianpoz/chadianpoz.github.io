<h2>3.1 Abstract</h2>

<p>The abundance of potentially habitable hypersaline environments in our solar system compels us to understand the impacts of high-salt matrices and brine dynamics on biosignature detection efforts. We identified and quantified organic compounds in brines from South Bay Salt Works (SBSW), where evapoconcentration of ocean water enables exploration of the impact of NaCl- and MgCl<sub>2</sub>-dominated brines on the detection of potential biosignature molecules. In SBSW, organic biosignature abundance and distribution are likely influenced by evapoconcentration, osmolyte accumulation, and preservation effects. Bioluminescence assays show that adenosine triphosphate (ATP) concentrations are higher in NaCl-rich, low water activity (<em>a</em><sub>w</sub>) samples (<em>&lt;</em>0.85) from SBSW. This is consistent with the accumulation and preservation of ATP at low<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> as described in past laboratory studies. The water-soluble small organic molecule inventory was determined by using microchip capillary electrophoresis paired with high-resolution mass spectrometry (µCE-HRMS). We analyzed the relative distribution of proteinogenic amino acids with a recently developed quantitative method using CE-separation and laser-induced fluorescence (LIF) detection of amino acids in hypersaline brines. Salinity trends for dissolved free amino acids were consistent with amino acid residue abundance determined from the proteome of the microbial community predicted from metagenomic data. This highlights a tangible connection up and down the “-omics” ladder across changing geochemical conditions. The detection of watersoluble organic compounds, specifically proteinogenic amino acids at high abundance (<em>&gt;</em>7 mM) in concentrated brines, demonstrates that potential organic biomarkers accumulate at hypersaline sites and suggests the possibility of long-term preservation. The detection of such molecules in high abundance when using diverse analytical tools appropriate for spacecraft suggests that life detection within hypersaline environments, such as evaporates on Mars and the surface or subsurface brines of ocean world Europa, is plausible and argues such environments should be a high priority for future exploration.</p>

<h2>3.2 Introduction</h2>

<p>The ability of salts to preserve biological material is well known and has been used by humans for thousands of years, from food storage to mummification [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-97'>97</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-98'>98</a>]. Microbial cells and their associated biomolecules can also be rapidly preserved in brines and salt inclusions [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-16'>16</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-99'>99</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-100'>100</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-101'>101</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-102'>102</a>]. For example, while adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is generally considered a short-lived biomolecule indicative of extant microbial activity, hypersaline culture experiments and studies of ATP in hypersaline anoxic basins have demonstrated the preservation and increased abundance of ATP in environments with high ionic concentrations [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-103'>103</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-104'>104</a>]. Most moderate halophiles are believed to be salt-out strategists and accumulate and synthesize organic solutes such as amino acids as an adaptation to osmotic stress [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-105'>105</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-106'>106</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-107'>107</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-108'>108</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-15'>15</a>].</p>

<p>Water activity (<em>a</em><sub>w</sub>), a unitless measure of water availability and a plausible indicator of habitability, has a strong influence on the production of molecular biosignatures and their preservation [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-14'>14</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-109'>109</a>]. The<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> limit of microbial activity (cell division or metabolism) has been sought theoretically, in the lab, and in nature [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-110'>110</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-111'>111</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-112'>112</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-113'>113</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-114'>114</a>]. Furthermore, chaotropicity, a measure of the biomolecular disordering imparted by a solute, has become commonly used in descriptions of brine environments as well. Certain salts, such as MgCl<sub>2</sub>, have greater chaotropic properties than others and thus, at high concentration, disproportionately disrupt hydrogen bonding, cellular functions, and ultimately cell survival [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-115'>115</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-116'>116</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-99'>99</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-117'>117</a>].</p>

<p>While the presence of extant liquid water on extraterrestrial planetary surfaces has been debated [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-36'>36</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-38'>38</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-118'>118</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-37'>37</a>], salt deposits and other hypersaline environments likely formed from water-rock reactions and subsequent evaporation [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-119'>119</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-120'>120</a>]. The presence of perchlorate salts in the martian regolith could also lower the eutectic temperature of surface waters allowing liquid water to briefly exist [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-121'>121</a>]. Droplets observed on the legs of the Phoenix lander and recurring slope lineae, if caused by liquid water, would require this perchlorate-based freezing point suppression [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-35'>35</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-122'>122</a>]. Additionally, Gale and Jezero craters may have held briny fluids, which, as water concentrated, ultimately resulted in evaporites [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-52'>52</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-123'>123</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-124'>124</a>]. Recent evidence from the ChemCam instrument aboard the Curiosity rover indicates the presence of hydrated sulfate minerals in Gale crater—this in addition to previous remote-sensing evidence for paleolake-deposited hydrated magnesium sulfates in Columbia crater [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-125'>125</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-122'>122</a>]. Beyond Mars, surface disruptions from potential endogenic activity on Jupiter’s moon Europa, such as chaos terrain and lenticulae, show signatures of Mg<sup>2+</sup>- and Na<sup>+</sup>-bearing brines within or below the ice shell [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-126'>126</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-127'>127</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-128'>128</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-129'>129</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-130'>130</a>]. Sodium-rich particles were detected by Cassini’s Cosmic Dust Analyzer in the Enceladus plume, which is likely sourced from a subsurface saline liquid water ocean [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-63'>63</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-12'>12</a>]. The organic-rich dwarf planet Ceres hosts surface salt deposits and is hypothesized to host subsurface brine as well, either in the pore space of the crust or as a distinct ocean layer [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-13'>13</a>]. Therefore, the behavior and detectability of organics in terrestrial brines are relevant to a host of extraterrestrial targets in the search for astrobiologically relevant chemistry.</p>

<p>Mars analog research has a rich literature describing arid, low <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> deserts such as the Atacama Desert in Chile [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-131'>131</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-132'>132</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-133'>133</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-134'>134</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-135'>135</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-30'>30</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-136'>136</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-137'>137</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-138'>138</a>] and the McMurdo Dry Valleys of Antarctica [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-139'>139</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-140'>140</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-141'>141</a>]. Concurrently, microbiology research in aqueous low<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> environments has been active for over four decades [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-142'>142</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-143'>143</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-144'>144</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-145'>145</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-146'>146</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-147'>147</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-148'>148</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-149'>149</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-150'>150</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-151'>151</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-152'>152</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-153'>153</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-104'>104</a>]. Many studies of the extreme limits to endolithic and halophilic life have encouraged the notion that signs of life may be best detected in higher<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> “special regions” where life may thrive [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-28'>28</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-154'>154</a>]. Parallel work has shown that salt fosters habitable environments [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-155'>155</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-156'>156</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-157'>157</a>] that catalyze origin-of-life chemistry [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-158'>158</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-159'>159</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-160'>160</a>], potentially facilitate cell survival through slower freezing [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-161'>161</a>], and allow the existence of liquid water on Mars [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-121'>121</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-34'>34</a>]. To summarize, while high <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> regions are important targets in the search for extant life, we hypothesize that accumulation and preservation of biomolecules associated with low<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> could make detectability of life (extant or extinct) greater in evaporitic settings.</p>

<p>The examination of analog sites on Earth can elucidate key processes and facilitate development of biosignature detection strategies. This can be done by determining biosignature concentration and preservation pathways as well as instrument limitations [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-162'>162</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-163'>163</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-164'>164</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-129'>129</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-165'>165</a>]. The 2008 NASA Astrobiology Roadmap defines a biosignature as an “object, substance, and/or pattern whose origin specifically requires a biological agent” [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-166'>166</a>]. Amino acids are ubiquitous in the solar system, from the building blocks of proteins for all life on Earth to high abundances in some types of meteorites and asteroids [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-167'>167</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-168'>168</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-169'>169</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-170'>170</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-7'>7</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-24'>24</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-171'>171</a>], and thus do not suffice as biosignatures directly. However, certain distributions of amino acids, such as enantiomeric excess or homochirality and predominant alpha structure, could be strong indicators of biological activity in extraterrestrial environments [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-73'>73</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-72'>72</a>]. While nonbiological symmetry-breaking processes exist, homochirality continues to be, when combined with other lines of evidence, a putative strong indicator of amino acid biogenicity. Another terrestrial biosignature molecule, ATP, is an energy carrier ubiquitous to life on Earth that is highly evolved and thus less likely to be conserved across planetary systems. Due to its hydrolysable, high-energy structure, and the abundance of microbial consumers and thus ATPase enzymes, ATP has a limited lifetime in most terrestrial environments [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-172'>172</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-173'>173</a>]. The ubiquity, yet rapid, decomposition of ATP enables its presence and quantity to be used as an indicator of microbial activity in many contexts, ex-cept hypersaline regions where its dynamics are complex and detection by enzyme-based assays has historically been challenging [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-103'>103</a>].</p>

<p>We studied the buildup of small, polar organic compounds from a biosignature detection perspective in brine samples taken from South Bay Salt Works (SBSW), a saltern in Chula Vista, CA, where seawater is captured and evaporated in a series of ponds until saturation occurs and salt crystalizes. These studies aimed to overcome presumed limitations of organic analysis in brines. Thus, we use geochemical parameters such as <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> and ion concentration to correlate biosignature compound concentration and consider the impact of these brines. Despite previous studies, few specifics are known about the fate of organic molecules in brines. To address ATP and amino acid dynamics in evaporative brine systems, we quantitatively analyzed dissolved free proteinogenic amino acids with capillary electrophoresis–laser induced fluorescence (CE-LIF) and ATP with a near-field bioluminescence assay. We also qualitatively detected amino acids, osmolytes, nucleobases, nucleosides, and polypeptides by using microchip CE paired to high-resolution orbitrap mass spectrometry (µCE-HRMS).</p>

<h2>3.3 Materials and Methods</h2>

<h3>3.3.1 Sample site</h3>

<p>Samples were collected from the SBSW (Fig. 3.1) in August 2019 and October 2020. Brine was collected into 50 mL polypropylene centrifuge tubes using either a dipping method or through a sample-rinsed peristaltic pumping tube attached to a 2 m long aluminum pole to extend the sampling range away from the edge of the ponds. Samples were analyzed via ATP assay within 24 h of collection. Samples were then stored at room temperature (RT) in their original tubes until CE-LIF and µCE-MS analyses were performed in 2022. Samples are denoted by site number and by year, that is, samples from site 10 in the year 2020 are denoted 20-10 in the following figures.</p>

<h3>3.3.2 ATP Bioluminescence assay</h3>

<p>Samples were analyzed using reagents and procedures from the ATP Bioluminescence Assay HSII kits from Roche Diagnostics (Basel, Switzerland), along with Tris and EDTA buffers (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) as described elsewhere [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-162'>162</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-174'>174</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-175'>175</a>]. Liquid samples were vortexed with 1 mL of buffer (100 mM Tris and 4 mM EDTA) and boiled for 5 min to lyse cells (Fig. 3.2). Samples were then returned to RT and centrifuged for 5 min before the supernatant was removed, mixed with the luciferase reagent, and analyzed with the portable Merk HY-LiTE 2 luminometer (Appendix Fig. B.1a). Each analysis was preceded by the generation of a standard curve. Standard curves were prepared in triplicate with stock solutions of ATP prepared in 100 mM Tris and 4 mM EDTA at 15 concentrations ranging from not detectable (1 x 10-14 M) to instrument saturation (1 x 10-7 M) and analyzed identically to post-lysed samples. The regime from 1 x 10-13 to 1 x 10-8 was used for quantitation with a power–law relationship fitted with an R<sup>2</sup> value of 0.999. A set of samples (denoted in this work as filtered) was passed through a 0.2 mm filter, and the filtrate was analyzed. All samples in this study were unfiltered unless otherwise stated. Sample dilution was optimized against salt interference by evaluating yield at sample dilutions of 1:2, 1:10, and 1:100 in Tris buffer.</p>

<h3>3.3.3 CE-LIF analysis</h3>

<p>To prepare samples for CE-LIF analysis, polypropylene tubes containing brine samples were first agitated to homogenize the sample, then sub-sampled into 1.5 mL aliquots, and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 5 min to remove large particulates that may have interfered with capillary injection. Unfiltered samples were used for these analyses to simplify the analysis and analogize the method to what is possible in spaceflight. Sodium tetraborate, the diluent and electrophoretic buffer, was recrystallized 5x in a HEPA-filtered clean room and dissolved into 3x-distilled MilliQ-filtered water. Then, each sample was diluted in 70 mM of sodium tetraborate and reacted with 2 mM carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE)</p>

<p>in dimethylformamide in darkness at RT for 24 h (Appendix Fig. B.1b). Post-reaction sample dilution was optimized through analyses of 1:1 (50 µL sample, 49 µL buffer, 1 µL dye), 1:10 (10 µL sample, 89 µL buffer, 1 µL dye), and 1:100 (1 µL sample, 98 µL buffer, 1 µL dye) dilutions. Sample dilution of 1:10 provided the highest recoverable concentration of dissolved free amino acids (DFAA), coupled with a lower rate of salt-induced capillary failure. Analysis was performed using a CE-LIF capillary zone electrophoresis method [seaton2023oceans] (Fig. 3.2) targeting 17 neutral and basic proteinogenic amino acids. This provided quantitative compositional information for DFAA in hypersaline samples. Separations were performed using a fused silica capillary from Polymicro Technologies (Phoenix, AZ) cut to a length of 60 cm (50 cm effective length) on a SciEx P/ACE MDQ system (Framingham, MA) paired with an LIF module and 488 nm laser. The sample was injected using a differential pressure of 0.4 psi for 5 s and separated in a buffer composed of 70 mM sodium tetraborate (Alfa Aesar, Ward Hill, MA) using a 30 kV applied voltage (0.5 kV cm-1). Electropherogram data were extracted from ASCII files obtained from the instrument and initially plotted using custom python script. Data processing and figure plotting were performed using PeakFit v4.12 (Systat Software, Inc., San Jose, CA) and OriginPro 2022 (OriginLab Co., Northampton, MA) software, respectively.</p>

<h3>3.3.4 µCE-MS analysis</h3>

<p>We used µCE-MS to identify small polar compounds in a broader manner than the primary amine-specific CE-LIF analyses and the ATP-specific assay (Fig. 3.2), enabling putative ID of compounds, which could not be detected with CE-LIF.</p>

<p>Samples were analyzed on a ZipChip system by 908 Devices (Boston, MA) paired with a QExactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer by Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA). The background electrolyte was composed of 68% water, 20% isopropanol, 10% acetonitrile, and 2% formic acid. The dilutant was composed of 80% methanol, 20% water, and<em> &lt;</em>1% ammonium acetate. Both were acidified immediately before use with a 50% volumetric mixture of formic acid in water. All water used was MilliQ-filtered to 18 MΩ-cm resistivity, and all solvents were HPLC grade. Brines were aliquoted and then diluted in a 1:4 (10 µL sample:40 µL acidified dilutant) mixture. Microchip conditioning steps were performed as described in the 908 devices procedure for water-soluble small molecules. A series of analyses at different dilution ratios showed 1:4 to be most effective for improving the diversity of visible compounds. An injection of 4.0 nL was electrophoretically separated for 2 min in a field of 1000 V cm<sup>-1</sup> using a High Speed ZipChip (908 Devices, Boston, MA) with a standard (non-pressure assist) injection. The MS analysis was run with a resolving power setting at 17,500 m/Dm at 500 m/z in full-scan mode with a scan speed of 12 Hz. The data were collected using Xcalibur 3.0 software (Thermo Scientific) and analyzed using a target list of relevant expected compounds in Skyline software [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-176'>176</a>].</p>

<h3>3.3.5 Physical characteristics</h3>

<p>Dissolved metals and ions (Al, B, Br, Ca, Cl, Co, Fe, Fl, Mg, Mn, Mo, Ni, K, Se, Si, S, SO<sub>4</sub>, Na, Zn) determined by ACZ Laboratories (Steamboat Springs, CO) for samples collected in 2019 are reported in the work of Klempay et al. (2021) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>]. Total dissolved solids (TDS) were calculated from these measurements. Metals and sulfur were determined with inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometry [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-177'>177</a>] and ions with ion chromatography [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-178'>178</a>]. <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> was determined <em>in</em> <em>situ</em> using an Aqualab 4TE chilled dewpoint meter (Addium Inc., Pullman, WA) to a precision of ±0.003. The geochemical parameters for each sample are included in Appendix Table B.2.</p>

<h3>3.3.6 Statistical methods</h3>

<p>Student’s<em> t</em>-tests (one-tailed, heteroscedastic) were performed between pairs of samples using three replicate ATP values per sample to determine the statistical significance of the difference in ATP concentration in the samples. Correlation matrices were created to elucidate trends of geochemical parameters and individual organic compound concentrations and depict Pearson <em>R</em><sup>2</sup> correlation (Fig. 3.3a and Appendix Fig. B.3). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on DFAA content quantified from CE-LIF data and displayed against clustered geochemical classifications for sample sites to map links between geochemical class and individual amino acids. All hierarchical clustering and PCA were performed with Ward clustering with Euclidian distance measure in Metaboanalyst 5.0 [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-179'>179</a>].</p>

<h2>3.4 Results</h2>

<h3>3.4.1 Ion content and brine characteristics</h3>

<p>The SBSW samples covered a range of ionic concentrations and<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> (0.393–0.979). Those data (the pH, <em>a</em><sub>w</sub>, [Na<sup>+</sup>], [Mg<sup>2+</sup>], and [Cl<sup>-</sup>]) were used to generate the clustered heatmap of geochemical data (Fig. 3.3b). The samples fall into three distinct groups according to this classification: those with <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> <em>&gt;</em>0.9 and low salinity (annotated as “low”); those with <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> <em>&gt;</em>0.5 to<em> &lt;</em>0.9, which have [Na<sup>+</sup>]<em> &gt;</em>31,800 mg L<sup>-1</sup>; and those with<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> <em>&lt;</em>0.5, which have [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] <em>&gt;</em>75,000 mg L<sup>-1</sup>. These classes correspond well to those identified by Klempay et al. (2021), conduit, which is seawater-like, NaCl brine, and MgCl<sub>2</sub> brine, which were manually classified and categorize each site identically to our study.</p>

<p>Pearson correlations between chemical species and geochemical concentrations were computed to assess relationships that may elucidate production or preservation pathways. These correlation coefficients were also calculated to establish correlations between pairs of ion concentrations (Fig. 3.3a) in order to understand the evapoconcentrative history and ion relationships within the saltern. Correlation was found to be 0.98 for [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] versus [Br<sup>-</sup> ] and -0.96 for [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] versus<em> a</em><sub>w</sub>, while [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] versus [Na<sup>+</sup>] has a weaker correlation of -0.3. [Br<sup>-</sup>] is a known tracer of evapoconcentration of seawater [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-180'>180</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-181'>181</a>]. Therefore, [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] can be considered a conservative marker of both evapoconcentration and<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> in this sample set, and Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich sites represent highly evapoconcentrated sites. Additionally, pH is more acidic in these sites, and it increases with increasing evapoconcentration as expected [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-14'>14</a>].</p>

<p>Our samples from SBSW have salinities (measured in TDS) that range from that of native bay water (36349.00 mg L<sup>-1</sup>) to saturated NaCl (370000.00 mg L<sup>-1</sup>) and saturated MgCl<sub>2</sub> (400928.10 mg L<sup>-1</sup>). Sampling this large gradient in ionic concentration allowed us to interrogate the interactions between salts and organics in extremely salt-rich environments and how salt concentration may aid or hinder biosignature detection efforts.</p>

<h3>3.4.2 ATP analyses of samples</h3>

<p>ATP concentration found in the brine samples from SBSW in years 2019 and 2020 was plotted against physical parameters <em>a</em><sub>w</sub>, [Na<sup>+</sup>], [Mg<sup>2+</sup>], and pH (Fig. 3.4). Standard deviation of the ATP analyses represents variations in triplicate subsamples and thus the heterogeneity of the sample and not variability in the analytical method. ATP concentration in the unfiltered brine samples from SBSW was observed to be consistently low (not ex-ceeding 5 nM) in brines with Mg<sup>2+</sup> concentrations up to 1.3 M, Na<sup>+</sup> concentrations up to 0.5 M, and <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> between 1.0 and 0.9. ATP concentration in pH 8–9 samples was low ex-cept for one point in site 12. These ATP concentrations rose to 20–60 nM in sites where Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Na<sup>+</sup> concentration increased up to 3.0 M and<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> decreased to 0.66. In this higher salinity regime, larger differences were observed in the ATP concentration measured for the triplicate subsamples of each sample. In the Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich brines (from sites 4 and 5 in both years), the ATP assay failed, likely due to known Mg<sup>2+</sup> inhibition of the assay. Our data show increasing ATP concentration with respect to increasing salt concentration and decreasing <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> until the assay fails near 3 M Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Na<sup>+</sup> (Fig. 3.4). Both filtered and unfiltered brines were analyzed in this study. Some samples (denoted as red circles) were filtered to remove cellular material or particulates<em> &gt;</em>0.2 mm in size. These filtered samples also showed increases in ATP concentration with brine concentration, though smaller, and in the higher salinity ranges had smaller variation among subsamples (shown in standard deviation) than the unfiltered. There was an increase in filtered sample ATP, which peaked</p>

<h2>3.5 Discussion</h2>

<p>Amino acid identification and quantitation with CE-LIF were easiest in samples with higher concentrations of DFAA despite their higher salt concentration, lower pH, and higher ionic strength. The low limits of detection and high sensitivity afforded by this method represent the state of the art in analysis of DFAA in native unprocessed hypersaline samples, as the high salt concentration can be accommodated by a simple dilution of the sample into running buffer (Fig. 3.2) [seaton2023oceans].</p>

<p>DFAA concentration is more strongly correlated to [Br<sup>-</sup>], a known marker of evapoconcentration [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-180'>180</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-181'>181</a>], than any other parameter (Appendix Fig. B.3). The extreme increase</p>

<figure><img src='/thesis/03-08.webp' alt='Figure' loading='lazy'></figure>

<p>in concentration of DFAA at Mg<sup>2+</sup> concentrations<em> &gt;</em>3.5 M (Appendix Fig. B.2) can thus be mostly attributed to evapoconcentration, as [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] and [Br<sup>-</sup>] are correlated as well. However, it is likely that evapoconcentration does not entirely dictate the DFAA compositions of these brines. In the more extreme brines, osmolyte synthesis due to ionic stress, or, furthermore, a lack of metabolic consumption of DFAA due to decreased cellular activity, could contribute to the sudden increase in DFAA concentration. This could occur as the brine’s salt concentrations and<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> push toward and then past the previously predicted<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> limit for life [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-187'>187</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-188'>188</a>]. Due to sample dilution changing the osmotic pressure experienced by the microbes and the<em> &gt;</em>1 year storage of the samples before CE analysis, we suspect cell lysis has occurred. Thus, we chose to limit sample preparation in the CE analyses to address this technique’s biosignature detection capability, as limited sample preparation is beneficial for spaceflight [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-189'>189</a>] as a strategy to reduce instrument complexity. Since the general stability of amino acids in solution is on timescales of months to years [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-167'>167</a>], preservation of DFAA could not be assessed here.</p>

<p>Our detection of an abundance of osmolyte compounds (betaine, ornithine, sarcosine, glycine, and proline) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-190'>190</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-106'>106</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-15'>15</a>] points to the presence of a salt-out response by microbes in these systems. The observed increases in glycine and proline with decreasing<em> a</em><sub>w</sub>, along with evidence of salt in osmolyte accumulation at the lowest <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> site, suggest their accumulation is in part due to osmotic stress (Table 3.1, Appendix Fig. B.5). We argue that in lower<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> samples, the free osmoprotectant amino acids represent an increasing sum of DFAA inventory, along with any amino acids made available for future protein synthesis.</p>

<p>Our detection of histidine as well as a series of highly concentrated alpha amino acids at site 4 represents the capability of our CE methods to detect signs of recently extant but inactive [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>], archaeal, and bacterial life in a biomass-rich yet low activity region (however, we must note that we did not target non-alpha amino acids or those not used by biology) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-191'>191</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-73'>73</a>]. Histidine constitutes a potential biosignature molecule in itself as it has not been feasibly synthesized under prebiotic conditions [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-192'>192</a>] or found in meteorites [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-72'>72</a>]. Nucleobase and nucleoside detection with this same method indicates that numerous targeted strategies may not be necessary to detect various compounds of astrobiological interest. Crucially, evapoconcentration aided the accumulation of organic matter to easily detectable levels. If salts and organics partition into the liquid portion in the freezing of brines similarly as in evapoconcentration, then these same implications would apply to icy moons, thus warranting further study of salt-rich regions of the surface of Europa.</p>

<p>A previous investigation of microbial community composition at SBSW using 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing showed that the relative abundance of many haloarchaea (e.g., Halorubrum and Halohasta) increases with decreasing <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> until just below <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> 0.7 [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>]. However, only Haloquadratum walsbyi and the halophilic bacterium, Salinibacter ruber, continue to increase past this <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> until the theoretical prokaryotic <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> limit of life at 0.54 is reached [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-188'>188</a>]. Beyond this limit, the relative abundance of Salinibacter decreases, while that of Haloquadratum increases further and reaches its maximum in the Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich brines. This increase in relative abundance beyond the known limit of cell division was observed despite nanoSIMS analyses, which indicates metabolic inactivity in these extremely chaotropic MgCl<sub>2</sub> brines [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-112'>112</a>]. To explain this effect, Klempay et al. (2021) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>] suggested that DNA is strongly accumulated and likely preserved in these brines, and the inventory of 16S rRNA genes beyond the limit of metabolic activity represents a preserved snapshot of the last active microbial community prior to crossing that threshold.</p>

<p>Preservation plays a role in other areas of the organic inventory, namely, for ATP. ATP concentration is commonly used as a signal of active biology due to its rapid rate of hydrolysis [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-162'>162</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-172'>172</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-173'>173</a>]. However, ATP has been preserved and/or produced at higher rates in highly saline media (NaCl-rich) in experimental studies of Serratia rubidaea under osmotic stress [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-104'>104</a>] and at high concentrations in situ at the deep sea hypersaline (NaCl-rich) anoxic Orca basin where there are extremely low cellular activity rates [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-103'>103</a>]. Tuovila et al. (1987) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-104'>104</a>] predicted that hydrolysis rates of ATP are limited by decreased<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> and osmotic stress induced by the accumulation of osmoprotectant molecules such as glycine and other free amino acids, to which they also attribute the need for higher intercellular ATP concentration. Additionally, the high ultraviolet (UV) exposure of the saltern is expected to increase the ATP accumulation though not its persistence [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-104'>104</a>]. Sites that have the highest salt concentrations are also presumably those that have evapoconcentrated for longer and, thus, have been subjected to higher UV exposure. Furthermore, in protein functional studies of Halobacterium salinarium, light-driven ATP generation by rhodopsins and ATPase inhibition by ions indicate that active buildup of ATP would occur in salterns [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-193'>193</a>]. Numerous computational and experimental studies have characterized the opposite behavior for magnesium solutions, Mg-catalyzed non-enzymatic ATP hydrolysis; however, this is only examined up to 200 mM [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-194'>194</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-195'>195</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-196'>196</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-197'>197</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-198'>198</a>]. These concentrations are much lower than the concentrations observed as SBSW. Based on ionic inhibition of ATP hydrolysis by NaCl at relevant concentrations, it is feasible to assume that ionic inhibition effects are occurring with high concentrations of MgCl<sub>2</sub> at SBSW as well.</p>

<p>We observed increasing ATP concentration with decreasing cell biomass and increasing salt concentration (Fig. 3.7). ATP concentration spikes significantly relative to total DFAA concentration in lower <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> samples despite a drop in cellular biomass (which we would expect to correlate in an activity assay). This suggests that, despite the assumption that ATP is being generated at lower levels in the mid-<em>a</em><sub>w</sub> (NaCl-rich) and high-<em>a</em><sub>w</sub> (MgCl<sub>2</sub>-rich) brines, it is being concentrated and potentially preserved, which allows for its detectability until failure due to analytical constraints. Future work will test the hypothesis that MgCl<sub>2</sub> concentration affects ATP detectability due to increased chaotropicity and, thus, denaturing of the assay’s luciferase protein.</p>

<p>Spatial distribution of biosignatures has been addressed by spatial sampling techniques on land [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-162'>162</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-199'>199</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-174'>174</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-175'>175</a>]; however, for aqueous environments, the story is more complex due to the convective nature of liquids. The results of our Student’s t-tests on ATP indicate that, at hypersaline salterns, heterogeneity and homogeneity in the organic compound payload are not clearly exclusive to any site type, but further work is warranted.</p>

<p>The ATP assay used for this work failed in the two highest ion concentration samples, which were obtained from sites 4 and 5. These samples are exceptionally chaotropic due to the high [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] and have the lowest<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> of any SBSW sample. Because the ATP assay depends on the catalytic activity of the enzyme luciferase, its inactivity at conditions well outside those typical of its biological context (in samples with high MgCl<sub>2</sub> concentration) is not unexpected, and the mechanisms of its deactivation are a subject of future molecular dynamics simulation and laboratory work, as well as sample preparation method development with desalting strategies. We also affirm the conclusion by Klempay et al. (2021) [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>] that chaotropic MgCl<sub>2</sub> salts can obscure whether cellular activity is occurring—in this case due to its impact on activity tracer ATP’s preservation, accumulation, and detection.</p>

<p>The clear grouping of our sample’s normalized DFAA concentrations with geochemical class (Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich, Na<sup>+</sup>-rich, seawater-like) in PCA (Fig. 3.8), as well as its hierarchical clustering (Fig. 3.6b), indicates that DFAA distribution here is affected by the most abundant salt type present, potentially due to the varying degree of ionic stress induced, in-creased chaotropicity, or reduced<em> a</em><sub>w</sub>. Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich sites are characterized by a predominance of glycine, while Na<sup>+</sup>-rich sites have lower asparagine and higher leucine and serine levels relative to their total. Because these data were sum-normalized per sample, these patterns can be compared independent of their overall changes in abundance induced by evapoconcentrative processes. The evapoconcentrative processes at SBSW are forced by human activities; however, the high solar radiation exposure and diurnal temperature cycling of the sites are not unexpected for past environments on Mars [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-52'>52</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-125'>125</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-123'>123</a>]. Europa, on the contrary, may experience dynamic refreezing of ice shells that would concentrate materials [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-144'>144</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-60'>60</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-59'>59</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-130'>130</a>].</p>

<p>We highlight that changes in relative enrichments of certain compounds entrained in different types of brines (or their resulting salt deposits) are at risk of being improperly attributed between analytical, taphonomic, kinetic, or biological processes in future in situ or returned sample investigations [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-200'>200</a>]. These data indicate that variability should in part be attributed to the evapoconcentrative characteristics of different salt types, but this does not rule out, as discussed below, the importance of microbial contribution changing due to geochemical characteristics.</p>

<p>The “-omics ladder” refers to a systems biology framework that examines the processing of information, integrating genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. A correlation coefficient of 0.54 indicates a moderate association between DFAA concentrations and theoretical amino acid residues in the total expressible proteome derived from the metagenomes at each site (Fig. 3.9). This suggests that across a range of<em> a</em><sub>w</sub>, the genes for proteins, not just the expression or post-translational characteristics, have a tangible relationship with the metabolites (DFAA) present. This connection marries the traditionally separate analytical domains of sequencing and chemical analysis within the -omics ladder, not at an individual or species scale but in an ecosystem.</p>

<p>It is possible that the proteins encoded in the metagenome sequenced from more saline environments are those that utilize salinity-resistant amino acid pairings (e.g., increasing number of salt bridges: aspartic or glutamic acid with arginine, or generally increasing the overall negative charge of the protein), thus influencing the DFAA inventory [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-201'>201</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-202'>202</a>]. Indeed, the denaturation of halophilic proteins in low salinities (i.e., once diluted for analysis) may play a role in the detectability of DFAA [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-203'>203</a>] even when no acid hydrolysis is performed before analysis, as in this case.</p>

<p>Because expression of the genome, or transcriptomics, and the actual protein inventory, or proteomics, are not studied here, there is still a large gap between the observed metagenomes and metabolomes (the small molecules within the sample) at each site. Not all genes in the metagenome are necessarily expressed equally or at the same time, which may explain why the observed correlation is not very strong. However, this appears to be the first instance of analytically detectable connection between the metabolome detected via chemical analyses (DFAA) and the overall constructed metagenome of the microbial community analyzed through sequencing tools [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-186'>186</a>] across varying geochemical</p>

<figure><img src='/thesis/03-09.webp' alt='Figure' loading='lazy'></figure>

<p>parameter-space. Incorporating proteomic and transcriptomic studies has the potential to <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> a closer link between these data [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-204'>204</a>] and describe microbial and ecosystem-wide response to geochemical changes, which will be investigated in future work.</p>

<p>With this connection established, we postulate that the systematic shift in the presence of amino acids (or other relevant biomolecules) across a set of samples of varying geochemical conditions in an extraterrestrial environment could be a stronger biosignature than individual detections of amino acid patterns. However, this is only if analytical, taphonomic (i.e., preferential preservation or fractionation), and abiotic reaction pathway variables, which may affect observed amino acid concentrations, are rigorously constrained in a variety of planetary surface environments. This gradient approach has more potential for discovering evidence of evolution or adaptation, a coveted observable upon the “Ladder of Life Detection” than any single data point and without the difficulty of time-resolved direct observation of microbes [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-205'>205</a>].</p>

<p>Overall, we show that the detectability of biosignature compounds is enhanced in the salt-rich planetary analog of SBSW, particularly the Mg<sup>2+</sup>-rich sites. Detectability in this instance is a function of the persistence of short-lived compounds such as ATP, accumulation of both short- and longlived compounds (including amino acids), and the low LOD and high salt tolerance of our CE-based analytical methods. Concentrated biosignature compounds persist at SBSW on short timescales compared with the millions or billions of years relevant in planetary environments; however, the properties observed corroborate the call for exploration of hypersaline sites in the search for extraterrestrial life.</p>

<p>If the chemical behaviors observed at SBSW (concentration, persistence, or increased osmolyte synthesis) occurred at Mars or at present-day ocean worlds like Enceladus, Europa, or Ceres, then salt-rich sites would be particularly well suited for the search for biosignatures. The search for life in high and low <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> surface regions presents conflicting narratives that must be weighed and considered for their relevance in future planetary life detection missions; where the former may indicate thriving life in a habitable window, the latter may indicate highly concentrated, preserved biosignature compounds from formerly habitable systems. Furthermore, finding gradients of salinity may better inform the providence and authenticity of organic biosignatures and can help isolate taphonomic and analytical effects on results. This will require instrumentation capable of processing and analyzing samples, both solid and liquid, with high concentrations of ionic compounds.</p>

<p>We demonstrate the ability of CE-LIF and µCE-MS in this arena and highlight the versatility of the CE separation method particularly for hypersaline liquid samples. Sample preparation is still a challenge in planetary exploration, and extraction of organics from a solid sample matrix is especially so [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-163'>163</a>]. CE is a liquid-based separation strategy that works on native liquids, including brines, with little modification (only dilution needed). While liquid sample processing has lower planetary spaceflight heritage than pyrolysis, the results of our study, and the advanced development of CE-LIF and CE-MS systems by other groups, still point to liquid handling and CE separation as a promising avenue for ocean worlds’ exploration in particular. Detection when using a fluorescent tag or with ESI (electrospray ionization) injection has been shown here to be effective for hypersaline samples. Thus, melting and ingesting salty ice samples on Europa, collecting plume materials from Enceladus, or using an extraction technique, such as subcritical water extraction, supercritical CO<sub>2</sub> extraction, or automated lipid extraction, all serve as options for CE separation and LIF or MS detection systems.</p>

<h2>3.6 Conclusions</h2>

<p>Our findings suggest that hypersaline regions that have formed via evaporative processes like those at SBSW hold great potential for biosignature detection, as the increasing concentration of the components of these brines causes the accumulation and enhanced detectability of organic biosignature molecules. We find decreasing <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> correlates with in-creasing biosignature detectability, but whether <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> or chaotropicity drives detectability alone is unclear and subject to further work. We argue that dissolved free amino acid patterns across salinity gradients may be used to observe stresses acting on the microbial population and, thus, for detecting the adaptation of a self-reproducing chemical system to an environment. We cannot extend these relationships to all evaporitic systems across multiple environments, and more must be learned about analytical and taphonomic influences on these patterns.</p>

<p>This invites future studies to assess natural lacustrine evaporitic systems and hypersaline anoxic basins for ATP and amino acid preservation and to explore spaceflight-relevant, salt-robust instrumentation. Our work supports the phenomenon of ATP preservation and its accumulation due to osmolyte accumulation despite the inhibition of the ATP assay in high [Mg<sup>2+</sup>] environments. Microbial community diversity and successional changes are closely linked with decreasing<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> at SBSW, and the persistence of DNA has already been demonstrated at low <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> SBSW sites [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-91'>91</a>]. While we find no evidence that life is active below the previously measured limit of life for cell division of <em>a</em><sub>w</sub> = 0.635, recent work suggests that cellular anabolic activity may be detectable until an<em> a</em><sub>w</sub> of 0.540 [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-112'>112</a>].</p>

<p>Our study, which represents a complementary targeted organic compound inventory of this saltern, is perhaps the first such study of any environmental brine at this level of MgCl<sub>2</sub> concentration and <em>a</em><sub>w</sub>. Further investigations will examine ATP and amino acid concentration and preservation in transient lakes and hypersaline anoxic basins and explore what mechanisms and boundaries may exist for it.</p>

<p>To enable life detection in extraterrestrial planetary environments, we must implement robust analytical tools in locations and on samples where biosignature compounds are present at sufficient concentrations to be detectable. Some instrument packages that in-corporate CE-LIF and CE-MS for biosignature detection are in advanced stages of development [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-206'>206</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-207'>207</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-184'>184</a>]. Recently, prioritized mission concepts [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-208'>208</a>] such as the Enceladus Orbilander and the Mars Life Explorer have the potential to implement CE-based instrumental payloads in the analysis of planetary environments for life detection [<a href='/thesis/references/#ref-62'>62</a>, <a href='/thesis/references/#ref-209'>209</a>]. In-stead of searching solely for regions where long-term preservation and abundant water may have been present, we can aim to send life detection–focused spacecraft to collect samples at concentrated salt deposits.</p>
