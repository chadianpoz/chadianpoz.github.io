<p><a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/24027184">The End of History</a> by Francis Fukuyama indicated to the public zeitgeist of the 1990s that western style democracy had “won” and would take over all nations on Earth in a process called globalization<a href="#footnote-1">1</a>. This was enabled by technological developments in telecommunications and transportation as well as <em>lassiez</em> <em>faire</em> economic policy. </p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nEVR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff3434b95-dd15-4f1c-8886-4285c89b1547_736x736.jpeg" alt="Cargo containers stacked under a blue sky"><figcaption>Where is this? Does it matter? Every port belongs to every nation.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Western control of global commodities and industrial markets interconnected by global shipping had existed since the late 1800s, but the 1990s  demonstrated the defeat of communist thought. The capitalist economy had proven itself so powerful an agent of thought that it would consume all things, ideologies and religions bending to meet it wherever it could be applied. Now, consumerism and the identity of the individual consumer forged in the 1950s and 1960s would make the era palatable to the global population. </p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!W_Ds!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc7837a11-5071-4ea0-934f-454d8a7e439d_736x495.jpeg" alt="Two tall buildings in a wheat field"><figcaption>Commodities like global agriculture, cultivated from downtown NYC.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Even the counterculture movements of the 1960s succumbed to their fate, being depowered by a realignment of their moral values with those of consumerism. Every counterculture element from Joan Baez to goth culture to could now be bought or sold<a href="#footnote-2">2</a>. The consumerist version of any idea was more spreadable and reliably survived for longer.</p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dEb6!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F386a541e-a8b9-423a-8892-eb8e2c11457d_600x584.jpeg" alt="An advertisement with city lights in the background"><figcaption>How underground is it if I can buy it at the store?</figcaption></figure>
<p>Glass skyscrapers went up in every major population center, enormous international airports connected all edges of the map, cell phones were given to the poorest rural farmers to enable “microloans”. Cars were made mandate for all Americans, the ultimate sign of individual expression through freedom of movement.</p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nJzn!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe882944a-c76c-41e7-aa31-01620e9d4ac1_735x502.jpeg" alt="An American Airlines jet on the runway"><figcaption>Boeing 747-100, say hello to the intercontinental commuter</figcaption></figure>
<p>All this to empower their participation in the economy as both consumers and producers. Who caused this behavior? GDP growth and unemployment minimization targets supplanted all other metrics for human value, as politicians focused on these, among others such as low oil and food prices, to garner constituent approval.</p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IyI4!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F84247263-1414-45e4-834e-237d7b0f2d78_736x586.jpeg" alt="Two tall buildings lit up at night"><figcaption>Architecture reflected the pure, towering values of exceptional capitalism.</figcaption></figure>
<p>From this arose an aesthetic vision, a comprehension of the perfect present, which represented the future. Now that we were in a state of development on the global scale we were pure, and we were free. Human nature had been brought to express its best qualities by the structures of capitalism, democracy, and justice. Human rights were as essential and real as air, and cultural respect was a given. Smiles are the universal language of globalization. Coca-cola helped with that, so did the olympics.</p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CRK5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa525112d-3da9-4d38-a6cf-63d2efa9f3d3_634x902.jpeg" alt=""><figcaption>Bill took his hands off the wheel, and gave the power to Wall Street, giving him time to work on cardio and meet new interns.</figcaption></figure>
<p>This aesthetic is demonstrated here in two collages I made using pinterest. It includes fields of wheat, puffy white clouds, and bright blue skies.</p>

<p>These are inspired by a few things including the first introductory chapter of an atlas my father had at home when I was a kid (filled with images of happy Africans, surely blessed by the growth of the global economy), the album art of Speaking of Now (2002) by the Pat Metheny Group, documentary stock footage of the early 2000s often seen on TV during that era (including slow motion long shot photography of vast crowds in NYC), and the objects of the age: Nokia phones, windmills, shipping containers.</p>
<figure><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!N4yS!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F29fe7fe2-b411-45c8-bfe1-6ad9a243a444_735x725.jpeg" alt=""></figure>
<p>Hopefully someday another person will agree with me that they’ve seen this aesthetic, that it’s not just a nostalgia contained within me, but was a real phenomenon. Until then, I browse the <a href="https://cari.institute/aesthetics">CARI</a>, waiting.</p>
